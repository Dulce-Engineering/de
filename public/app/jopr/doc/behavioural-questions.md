What they want: Conflict management without escalation, professionalism, finding common ground. Diplomatic honesty, focus on improvement not criticism.
- Situation:
 introduced formal code reviews. one developer holding back pull requests, and when he finally submitted large batches of code, he took review feedback personally and became very defensive.
- Task: 
unblock PR, eductae, nurture 
- Action:
Instead of hashing it out publicly in PR comments, I pulled him into one-on-one sessions to talk it through in person. I took the time to step back and explain the bigger picture—that reviews exist to protect the team as a whole, catch bugs early, and make shared ownership painless. To make it concrete and depersonalized, I walked through code samples side-by-side, clearly illustrating *why* certain patterns were recommended and how they improved maintainability.
To maintain momentum and keep his morale high, we agreed on a pragmatic compromise: we prioritized the critical items for that immediate release and flagged the non-blocking suggestions as technical debt for future refactoring sprints.
- Result: 
That shift in approach completely turned things around. Once he realized reviews weren't an attack on his competence, he started committing smaller, incremental changes much more frequently, and the review process became a natural, collaborative part of his daily workflow.
