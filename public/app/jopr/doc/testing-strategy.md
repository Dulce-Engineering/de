Building a trustworthy group of 5–10 beta testers requires focusing on **motivation over volume**. You don't want "freebie seekers" who sign up once and ghost; you want people actively suffering from the problem JOPR solves—job seekers, contract engineers, or recruiters who will genuinely stress-test the workflow.

---

### 1. Identify "High-Intent" Communities

Go where your target users are already complaining about the friction of applying for jobs, updating CVs, or tracking applications:

* **Reddit:** Target niche subreddits like `r/resumes`, `r/jobs`, `r/EngineeringResumes`, or `r/cscareerquestions`. Look for people asking for CV feedback or complaining about tedious job application forms.
* **Local Developer / Tech Meetups:** Connect with local communities (e.g., local developer user groups or Sydney Social Tech / AI builder meetups). Roomfuls of active job hunters, contractors, and engineers love testing new tools built by fellow devs.
* **Indie Hacker & Builder Circles:** Communities like `r/SideProject` or `r/indiehackers` contain builders who understand software and will give constructive, highly technical bug reports.

---

### 2. Add Friction to Filter for Quality

Counter-intuitively, making it slightly harder to join your beta eliminates casual users and isolates high-value testers:

* **Short Application Gate:** Don't just post an open link. Require a simple 3-question Google Form:
1. *Are you currently actively applying for jobs?* (Yes/No)
2. *How many applications do you submit a week?*
3. *What is your biggest frustration when tailoring your CV?*


* **1-on-1 Onboarding Call:** Offer a 15-minute quick setup call or personal demo in exchange for access. Users who agree to talk face-to-face or via video will give 10x better feedback than passive web users.

---

### 3. Direct 1:1 Outreach (The "Cold DM" Strategy)

Manual outreach yields the highest quality feedback:

* Search Reddit or LinkedIn for people posting about recent job hunts or struggles with tailoring resumes.
* **Send a brief, non-salesy message:**
> *"Hey [Name], saw your post about tracking/tailoring job applications. I built a privacy-focused tool designed to extract job specs and tailor CVs automatically without sending data to third parties. Looking for 5 active job hunters to test it out for free in exchange for honest feedback. Would you be open to trying it out?"*



---

### 4. Offer Meaningful Incentives

Money isn't always necessary; value and recognition work better for early testers:

* **Free Lifetime / Long-Term Access:** Give them $20–$50 worth of AI credits or a lifetime account once you launch paid tiers.
* **Direct Input on Features:** Tell them they get direct access to you (via a private Discord/Slack channel or direct email) to request features they want built.
* **Reciprocate:** Offer to review their CV manually or give feedback on their portfolio/code in return for their testing time.

---

### 5. Structure the Feedback Loop

Once you recruit your 5–10 testers, keep the feedback friction as low as possible:

* **In-App Feedback Button:** Put a persistent "Report Issue / Suggest Feature" button in the corner of JOPR that pre-fills user details.
* **Weekly 1-Question Check-In:** Avoid massive surveys. Send a quick single email: *"Did JOPR save you time on your applications this week? What was the most annoying bug you hit?"*
